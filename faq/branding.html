18 questions in Branding

What should a brand identity package include?

A useful brand identity package gives your team more than a logo. It should cover logo versions, colour values, typography, imagery direction, practical usage rules and templates for the channels you use. The right scope depends on whether you're starting from zero or updating an existing brand.

What should be included in brand guidelines?

Brand guidelines should specify logo sizes and spacing, colour values, typefaces, imagery, tone of voice and examples for common applications. They help different people create material that still looks like the same business.

What is brand identity and why does it matter?

Brand identity is how people recognise your business across your website, social media, advertising and documents. It brings together the logo, colours, typography, imagery and tone of voice. A consistent identity makes even a small business look more established and easier to remember.

What is a brand strategy for small businesses?

A small business needs to know who it serves, why customers should choose it and what it wants to be known for. Brand strategy turns those decisions into consistent positioning, messaging and creative choices so marketing is recognisable rather than improvised.

How do I define my target audience and value proposition?

Look at your best customers, the problems they need solved, their budget and why they choose you over alternatives. Your value proposition should state the audience, specific outcome and a credible reason to believe you—not just claim that your business is the best.

What is the difference between branding and marketing?

Branding defines what a business stands for and how customers recognise it. Marketing brings that message to people through content, advertising, search and other channels. You need both: more exposure won't help much if customers cannot understand what makes the business different.

What makes a good business logo?

A good logo works at small sizes, in one colour and across different formats. Distinctive type or symbols matter more than decoration; the final assets should include suitable versions for websites, social profiles and documents.

Should a logo work in black and white?

Yes. A logo should remain recognisable in black and white because it may appear on invoices, packaging, uniforms, documents or single-colour print. Colour is part of the identity, but the shape and lettering should still work without it.

Why does a company need a brand style guide?

Without guidelines, different designers, staff and suppliers may use different fonts, logo versions and colours. A practical style guide reduces that inconsistency and helps campaigns, presentations, websites and printed materials look like one business.

When should a business consider rebranding?

Consider rebranding when your business has changed audience, offer or positioning, or when the current identity no longer reflects the company. Start with a brand review; sometimes a clearer message and refreshed assets are enough without replacing everything.

Can I rebrand without confusing existing customers?

Yes, if the transition is planned. Keep useful recognisable elements, explain the change and update your website, social channels, email templates and customer touchpoints consistently. Customers should still recognise the business they already trust.

How should a new brand identity be launched?

A launch should coordinate the new logo and guidelines with updated website pages, social profiles, advertising assets, email signatures and key customer materials. Announce what changed and why, then check for old branding that may still be visible online.

How do I position my business against competitors?

Positioning starts with the alternatives your customers are considering. Identify your audience, their most important need and a difference you can prove—such as a particular specialism, service model or outcome. Your website and campaigns should communicate that difference consistently.

What is a brand positioning statement?

A positioning statement identifies who a brand serves, what category it competes in, the main customer benefit and why that claim is credible. It's a working decision tool for your website, advertising and sales messaging rather than necessarily a public slogan.

How can brand positioning help premium pricing?

Customers pay more when they understand meaningful differences in quality, expertise, convenience or service. Positioning helps explain those differences with evidence and reduces the temptation to compete only on discounts. It cannot justify a premium without real value.

What is brand tone of voice?

Tone of voice describes how a brand sounds across its website, ads, captions and customer messages. Document clear word choices and examples, while allowing the level of formality to change by channel.

How do I keep a consistent brand message on every platform?

Define a few core messages, proof points, vocabulary rules and examples for different channels. A LinkedIn post can sound more professional than a Reel, but the underlying promise and brand personality should stay consistent. Reusable copy guidelines help different team members achieve this.

What is the difference between brand messaging and copywriting?

Brand messaging decides what your business needs to communicate—who it helps, the value it offers and why to believe it. Copywriting turns that strategy into actual website headlines, ads, emails and social captions tailored to each audience and action.
